# SparseCraft: Agentic Hardware-Software Co-Optimization for Sparse Computing 

Rajatabha Chakraborty *, M P Samartha *, Vedant Pahariya *, and Priyesh Shukla, Member, IEEE 

International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad, India email: priyesh.shukla@iiit.ac.in 

Abstract —Sparse-accelerator design spaces are usually searched against analytical models, so a design point is admitted on what a model predicts rather than on what the hardware does. SparseCraft closes that gap with a language model inside a closed CHIA loop. In each of 15 iterations the model reads the measured outcome of the previous one and edits the Chisel RTL, the memory configuration and the sparse-kernel schedule of a Gemmini accelerator through MCP tool servers, and no candidate counts until it has been checked for legality, elaborated, simulated cycle-accurately, checked bit-for-bit on every output against a golden reference, and synthesised. The harness turns each measurement into the next work order, a diagnosed bottleneck with matching strategy guidance, the history of tried designs and a score of the model’s own prediction, and a second model repairs changes that fail a gate. On a 512 ×512 GraphChallenge sparse-DNN layer the loop reaches 2.1 × fewer cycles, 9.8 × less off-chip traffic and 22.8% less area than the block-sparse Gemmini baseline, with 5.61 ×

higher modelled perf/W and 11.8 × lower EDP. The levers span three layers: a schedule that keeps the dense operand resident removes 9.8 × of the traffic, a zero-gated MAC and a zero-row skip unit that the model wrote in Chisel cut energy, and resizing the memories cuts area. Index Terms —LLM agents, hardware/software co-design, sparse accelerators, Gemmini, CHIA. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Language models write hardware description code that compiles and passes simulation [1], [2], [3], [4], set accelerator parameters [5], [6] and optimise accelerator kernels [7], but mostly generate once, are scored by an analytical model, or tune one layer. A co-design loop asks more: the model must read a measured outcome, change RTL, memory or software, and live with the consequences over many iterations, while the evaluation stays out of its reach. Sparsity makes the problem cross-layer: a block-sparse kernel skips the empty half of our workload’s blocks, but the occupied ones are only 6.25% dense, so 8,388,608 MACs are issued for 524,288 useful ones, and recovering that waste needs RTL, memory and schedule changes together. SparseCraft is such a loop, built on CHIA [8], an open-source framework for agentic hardware/software co-design, for sparse matrix multiply on Gemmini [9], a Chisel-generated systolic array in Chipyard [10]. A. Structure 

Algorithm 1 lists one run; its stages are the nodes of Fig. 1. Iteration 1 evaluates the unmodified baseline. Each later iteration first asserts that the immutable harness inputs (legality rules, Pareto admission, energy model, metrics parser and kernel) still hash to their recorded values ( N74 ). The proposer then edits the tree and the harness evaluates it. An admitted design becomes the parent of the next iteration; a rejected one is rolled back exactly, RTL included, and recorded with the reason it failed. The budget is fixed at 15 iterations, so the counter, not the model, ends the run. 

B. The proposer 

The proposer ( N10 ) is Claude Opus 5 [11] running as a Claude Code agent [12]; one call is one agentic session of several tool calls. Every built-in tool is removed, so its only actions are three MCP tools served by CHIA: a bash tool inside the Chipyard build container, restricted to three writable files (the design-point parameters, the PE and the zero-bitmap unit), a status tool that returns the last measured design, and a history tool that returns every design tried so far with its result.

The proposer’s system prompt states the workload, the two sanctioned sparsity techniques and how they interact, the objectives, and the legality rules up front. Each iteration’s work order then supplies, in order, the parent’s design state, the measured diagnosis and counters, the strategy modules selected for the diagnosed bottleneck, a numbered procedure (read before writing, make one change, verify, compile), and the model’s own untried candidates. The answer must end in three parsed sections: a list of candidates ,the one mutation implemented and compiled, and a prediction 

of better, flat or worse for time, energy, area and clock, which the harness scores against the measurement. The repairer’s prompt adds a debugging method (validate along the gate ladder, one change at a time), a failure playbook per verdict and a confidence protocol: fix only a root cause it can name, otherwise report NOT ACTIONABLE .

D. Design state and legality 

The design is a typed record of 28 hardware fields (array geometry, scratchpad and accumulator capacity and banking, DMA and queue parameters, the sparsity mechanisms) and 6 schedule fields; a schedule-only change reuses the cached elab-oration. Legality ( N20 ) checks 27 named rules in microsec-onds before anything is built: Gemmini’s generator invariants, capacity and scheduling couplings between hardware and kernel, a sparsity-metadata budget and an area budget. F. Admission, diagnosis and repair 

N60 admits a design that no admitted design matches or beats in time, energy and area (Pareto non-dominance). N61 

labels the bottleneck (memory, compute, idle or balanced) from the DMA-wait and array-activity counters, and the label selects the strategy modules of the next work order. A failed gate is classified (N71 ); infrastructure faults never reach a model. The repairer ( N73 ) receives the shrunk evidence (N72 ), the proposer’s stated mechanism, both design states and earlier attempts, and returns a fixed tree, which re-runs the whole ladder, or reports NOT ACTIONABLE with a root cause for the next proposer. Reverting repairs are detected, and attempts are capped at three per iteration. III. E XPERIMENTAL SETUP

A. Trajectory 

Fig. 2 shows every iteration and Fig. 3 the design held after each. The model’s first move was in software: keeping all of 

X resident in the scratchpad (iteration 2) cut off-chip traffic 9.8 × and cycles 1.34 × at zero area. The two RTL mechanisms followed: T-A (iteration 3) cost 1.7% area for 6.6% less energy and T-B (iteration 4) 4.2% area for 17.5% less, neither changing a cycle. With X resident the scratchpad was over-provisioned, and halving it (iteration 8) cut cycles from 79,433 to 50,862 (36%), which the model had not expected; a second halving and a smaller accumulator (iterations 9 and 15) cut area further, while finer ZBU granules (iterations 10, 11) and a schedule change (12) did not improve perf/W. Five proposals were rejected. A 32 ×32 array (iteration 5) compiled and simulated but was wrong in 32,674 of 32,768 outputs, since the kernel’s 16 ×16 blocking admits no other array size; iterations 6 and 7 repeated it (duplicates), and 13 and 14 failed legality. The model’s directional predictions held in 30 of 36 cases. 

B. Time breakdown 

The run took 328 minutes of active time (21.9 per iteration, Fig. 4). Charging each instant to the highest-priority stage running, RTL simulation takes 75% and the 17 LLM sessions 20%. Synthesis (95 minutes) always ran beside a simulation, off the critical path, and rejected iterations cost only their LLM time, except iteration 5.

V. C ONCLUSION 

SparseCraft places a language model inside a closed CHIA loop, where over 15 iterations it co-designed the RTL, memory configuration and software schedule of a sparse Gemmini accelerator, driven by measured feedback rather than an an-alytical model. The loop seals the model to three writable files and a few MCP tools, structures its reasoning through composed prompts, work orders built from measured feedback and parsed predictions, and admits a design only after legality, compilation, elaboration, cycle-accurate simulation, bit-exact equivalence on every output and synthesis, with a second model repairing changes that fail a gate. Working across three layers, the model kept the dense operand resident in software, wrote a zero-gated MAC and a zero-row skip unit in Chisel, and resized the memories, reaching 2.1 × fewer cycles, 9.8 × less off-chip traffic, 22.8% less area, 5.61 ×

higher modelled perf/W and 11.8 × lower EDP than the block-sparse baseline, with its directional predictions right in 30 of 36 cases. Every reported number comes from a design that was built, simulated, checked on every output and synthesised. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
