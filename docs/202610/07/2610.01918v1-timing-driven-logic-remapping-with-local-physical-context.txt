# Timing-Driven Logic Remapping with Local Physical Context 

Zijian Jiang, Hongyang Pan, Cunqing Lan, Keren Zhu ∗

State Key Laboratory of ASIC and System, Fudan University, Shanghai, China 

Abstract —The timing behavior of a mapped circuit depends on both its logic implementation and the physical environment in which that implementation is realized. Revisiting mapping decisions after placement therefore requires a search procedure that accounts for surrounding timing constraints, fanout loads, and interconnect effects. We study local remapping in this setting and develop a framework that couples discrete mapping search with physical implementation feedback. Timing-critical regions are isolated through bounded windows whose interfaces retain the context of the surrounding circuit. Within each window, a mixed-integer formulation jointly selects logic cuts, signal polarities, and library cells under a delay model informed by estimated locations and interconnect parasitics. A continuous relaxation filters the search space before discrete optimization produces alternative implementations with similar modeled timing and dif-ferent structural choices. These implementations are reconstructed and assessed through legalization, routing-based parasitic esti-mation, and timing analysis. Physically validated improvements are incorporated into the design, and the updated context guides subsequent searches. The framework provides a systematic way to revisit local logic implementations while accounting for their interaction with an existing placement. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Timing closure remains one of the most iterative stages in physical design. Gate sizing and buffer insertion adjust delay by modifying cell drive strength or inserting repeaters, but do not explore alternative Boolean decompositions. When the original logic decomposition is suboptimal, the existing structure can limit the timing improvements achievable through these operations. Logic restructuring complements this toolkit by replacing combinational regions with functionally equivalent implementations that may offer better timing after physical realization. Applying restructuring to an already placed design provides access to the surrounding physical context, but also requires the new implementation to remain compatible with that context. Post-placement local restructuring raises two related chal-lenges. First, replacing gates introduces new instances that require placement and legalization. A larger region exposes more optimization opportunities, but may disrupt more of the established placement and alter the loads seen by neighboring gates. Extraction must therefore balance optimization scope against physical disruption. Second, even a bounded region admits a combinatorial space of cut, phase, and cell choices. A mapping cost model, including one informed by physical information, may rank these choices differently from timing analysis after reconstruction, legalization, and parasitic updates.

A modeled improvement thus need not survive physical imple-mentation. Evaluating every alternative in the placed design is costly, motivating selective candidate generation followed by physical validation. Prior efforts connect logic optimization and physical design in several ways. Concurrent approaches interleave logic opti-mization with placement to account for their interaction [1]– [4]. Layout-driven synthesis also considers congestion during decomposition and mapping [5]. Physically aware technol-ogy mapping incorporates spatial information into mapping decisions: PigMAP uses primitive-gate placement to guide mapping [6], and PigMap2 extends the use of physical infor-mation in the mapping framework [7]. PigMap3 further studies incremental mapping with on-the-fly post-layout critical-path tracking [8]. CPA-Remap constructs local cones using critical paths and cell locations for physically aware remapping [9]. These approaches establish the relevance of physical context to mapping optimization. Our focus is a complementary question: how can the timing and load context of a placed design guide joint local mapping decisions while accounting for the physical effects of implementing those decisions? Our key insight is to guide local restructuring with the timing and load context of the placed design and estimates of candidate interconnect delay, while using physical evaluation to deter-mine candidate acceptance. Based on this insight, we propose Physically Contextualized Restructuring (PCR), which extracts bounded windows around timing-critical endpoints and jointly optimizes cut, phase, and library-cell choices. Reference timing and external pin loads retain the surrounding design context. Predicted gate positions provide wirelength and RC estimates for candidate scoring; these estimates remain fixed within each solve and can be refreshed using the selected topology. A linear programming (LP) relaxation screens windows for modeled improvement and restricts the mapping domain for integer optimization. Sparse and structurally distinct candidates are selected under a common modeled timing cap, reconstructed, and evaluated through legalization and global-routing-based timing analysis. Accepted candidates update the physical design and provide the reference context for subsequent iterations. Our main contributions are as follows:  

> •

A local mapping formulation that jointly models cut, phase, and cell choices within bounded windows, incor-porating estimated interconnect delays while preserving shared boundary signals and the surrounding timing con-text (Sections III-A–III-B).  

> •

A staged solution procedure that combines relaxation-arXiv:2610.01918v1 [cs.AR] 1 Oct 2026 bound screening and action-domain restriction with integer feasibility and timing optimization (Sections III-B–III-C).  

> •

A dual-representative generation strategy that selects sparse and structurally distinct mappings under a common modeled timing cap, enabling separate physical evaluation of alternatives with similar modeled timing (Section III-C).  

> •

An experimental evaluation showing that the complete PCR flow outperforms PigMap3 [8] with 86.7%/95.2% reductions in geometric-mean WNS/TNS violation mag-nitudes under position-aware postprocessing, and genetic and annealing with 48.6–49.2%/53.3–63.1% reductions under uniform postprocessing. It outperforms PigMap3 in WNS on seven of nine cases and each resynthesis baseline on six of seven cases, with one tie (Section IV). The source code is available at https://github.com/jzj3021/ PCR. II. P RELIMINARIES 

A. Logic Synthesis and Technology Mapping 

Logic synthesis optimizes a technology-independent repre-sentation, such as an And-Inverter Graph (AIG), before map-ping it to library cells. Cut-based mapping [10], [11] selects compatible implementations covering the required outputs, of-ten from a restricted set of priority cuts [12]. Delay-oriented methods include load-aware ExactMap [13] and learned delay prediction in AiMap+ [14]. PCR uses ABC [15] to obtain legal cut, phase, and cell alternatives and their dependencies, defining the discrete domain optimized in Section III-B.

C. Placement and Timing Closure 

Cell locations and interconnect geometry determine wire re-sistance and capacitance. Static timing analysis (STA) combines cell models with parasitics estimated from placement or global routing to compute arrival and required times. Setup slack is required time minus arrival time; negative slack indicates a violation. Worst negative slack (WNS) measures the largest setup violation, and total negative slack (TNS) sums negative endpoint slacks. 

D. Post-Placement Timing Optimization 

Post-placement timing optimization adjusts cell drive strengths and interconnect loads through sizing, buffering, and related transformations. Lagrangian-relaxation-based siz-ing [16], [17] optimizes cell sizes under timing constraints; BufFormer learns buffer-tree topology, sizes, and locations [18]; OpenROAD’s Resizer [19] provides cell and interconnect repair operations. PCR complements these techniques by replacing local logic with equivalent mappings, using estimated positions and interconnect delays for search and physical evaluation for acceptance. III. A PPROACH 

PCR consists of four stages: (1) timing-driven window extraction , which selects a violating endpoint and extracts a bounded logic region; (2) mapping formulation and LP screening , which estimates candidate positions and intercon-nect delays, models legal mapping choices, and screens for modeled improvement; (3) integer mapping and reconstruc-tion , which uses MIP to generate concrete candidate netlists; and (4) physical evaluation and iterative update , which vali-dates candidates through physical checks and global-routing-based timing evaluation before accepting a change. These stages repeat until timing is closed or the search budget is exhausted. 

A. Timing-Driven Window Extraction 

PCR extracts a bounded combinational window rooted at the worst-slack data endpoint, subject to limits on depth, instance count, and boundary inputs. Sequential and macro instances remain outside the window. The extracted context includes interface nets, reference STA timing, external pin loads, and the positions of existing cells, boundary sources, and external sinks. Boundary-source and external-sink positions anchor candidate position estimates. Signals that drive loads outside the window are retained as boundary outputs, preserving their functions and external connections without expanding the window to include all fanout instances. The extracted logic defines an action graph of legal cut–phase choices, fanin dependencies, and available cell variants. Re-quired outputs induce demands on internal nodes, and compat-ible actions are selected jointly to form a complete mapping.

D. Physical Evaluation and Iterative Update 

Predicted coordinates serve only the delay model. Each reconstructed candidate is applied to a separate copy of the current placed design, where actual placement and legalization determine its physical locations. Detailed placement legalizes the modified region, followed by placement checking, global routing, parasitic estimation, and STA. The candidate and reference are evaluated using the same timing constraints and physical settings. A candidate is accepted when WNS and TNS do not degrade beyond tolerance, at least one improves, and the composite cost decreases. The tolerances and cost function are specified in Section IV-A. If the candidate passes, we retain its design, recompute the search context, and restart endpoint selection from the worst slack. Otherwise, we retain the previous design, try any remaining representative, and then advance to the next endpoint. Iteration stops when setup timing is closed, the window limit is reached, or the wall-time budget expires. IV. E XPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

C ONCLUSION 

We presented PCR, a physically contextualized local restruc-turing method for post-placement timing optimization. PCR ex-tracts bounded windows around timing-critical endpoints while preserving their interfaces to the surrounding design. Within each window, PCR predicts gate positions and interconnect RC to jointly optimize cut, phase, and cell choices under a delay model frozen for each solve. The selected topology can refresh this model once; an LP relaxation screens for modeled improvement and guides action-domain restriction, followed by integer optimization to select sparse and structurally diverse candidate mappings. Candidates are reconstructed, legalized, and evaluated using global-routing parasitics before acceptance. Accepted updates guide subsequent window selection, enabling iterative optimization within a fixed computation budget. Exper-imental results show that the complete PCR flow outperforms PigMap3 [8] with 86.7% and 95.2% reductions in geometric-mean WNS and TNS violation magnitudes under the position-aware flow, and genetic and annealing with reductions of 48.6– 49.2% and 53.3–63.1% under the uniform flow.
