# MESH KV: A Network-on-Chip KV Cache Fabric for Scalable Transformer Decoding Accelerators 

Dong Liu *

University of California, Los Angeles Los Angeles, CA, USA pikeliu@ucla.edu 

Yanxuan Yu *

Columbia University New York, NY, USA yy3523@columbia.edu 

Abstract —Autoregressive transformer decoding is constrained by irregular key–value (KV) cache movement on tiled accelerators. Prior compression and DRAM-placement systems still concentrate traffic on centralized memory paths that bottleneck long-context serving. We present M ESH KV, a KV cache fabric that moves blocks as packetized flows over a lightweight N OC. It co-designs (i) T AKV affine striping to spread homes and cut hotspot load, (ii) M ARE multicast with verified duplicate suppression, and (iii) P AD , which overlaps prefetch, tile multiply, and streaming softmax behind credit-aligned FIFOs. Together they convert bisection back-pressure into useful KV transfer. On our 8×8

FPGA implementation with LLaMA-2-7B and Mistral-7B at 8K–32K, M ESH KV reduces interconnect traffic by up to 58 %,improves KV bandwidth utilization by 2.1×, and delivers up to 

1.9× multi-stream throughput. 

Index Terms —Transformer decoding, KV cache, network-on-chip, accelerator architecture, long-context LLM, multicast routing, spatial locality, FPGA 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

LLM decoding is “memory-bound,” but tiled accelerators also stall on on-chip data movement. Autoregressive attention scatters KV across a mesh built for dense GEMM, not all-to-few gather over a growing prefix [1]–[3]. ASICs, FPGAs, and chiplet meshes map compute to a 2-D PE array with private SRAM. Prefill amortizes tile hops; decode does not: one query row ( dh≈128 ) meets a KV slab that grows every token. A centralized design routes these fetches through a shared injection point, concentrating long-context KV traffic on the same bisection. 

Our approach. We treat the KV cache as a communication substrate : each step issues structured, layer-major reads that ad-mit co-designed placement, routing, and compute. M ESH KV is a N OC-centric fabric. Unlike DRAM paging or compression [3], [4], it optimizes how resident SRAM blocks traverse the mesh— multicast from each home tile, with prefetch overlapping matmul across credit boundaries.

KV cache management and serving. PagedAttention [3], FlexGen [4], and TinyServe [5] virtualize or select KV behind a serving-layer controller; M ESH KV distributes it inside a tiled accelerator with private SRAM. Quantization and eviction [6]– [11] shrink the bytes per step; M ESH KV complements these by improving residual transport. 

Attention hardware. FlashAttention [2], [12] and FlashDe-coding++ [13] tile GPU attention and softmax; ELSA [14] and SpAtten [15] exploit ASIC sparsity. M ESH KV targets on-chip communication; P AD uses online softmax in a credit-aligned pipeline. 

Networks-on-chip and FPGA accelerators. Classic N OCs and the Turn Model [16]–[18] supply deadlock theory; Simba [19] uses a 2-D mesh without KV-class flits. DFX [20], FlightLLM [21], and CXL-SpecKV [22] show HBM/CXL KV movement dominates FPGA decode; M ESH KV replaces point-to-point KV buses with an attention-aware fabric. III. B. Tiled accelerators and the spoke–hub pathology 

Tile-based accelerators (e.g., Simba [19], custom chiplets) map heads and layers across a 2-D PE mesh. Prefill carries dense GEMM traffic; decode issues width-dh gathers from wherever a KV block lives to wherever the compute tile sits. When KV sits at one logical memory controller, all such reads converge at the same injection point. The mesh degenerates to a spoke–hub: H requesters fan in to one hub, consuming 40–60% of link cycles on back-pressure (Figure 1). 

C. Observed locality in KV accesses 

An instrumented FP16 decoder on LLaMA-2-7B [23] at T ∈{8192 , 32768 } shows two regularities that motivate M ESH KV: 

Observation 1 (Layer-major temporal locality) . Within a decode step, layers are visited in order ℓ = 0 , . . . , L −1; a 

KV line is reused only at step t+1 , so the prefetch window is exactly L layer traversals. 

Observation 2 (Head-group spatial locality) . Heads that share a prefix span request identical segments (Mistral-7B GQA uses G=4 ; LLaMA-2-7B is multi-head). At T =32K , 78% of segment requests arrive within 12 cycles at the same router, so multicast coalescing does not require wide merge buffers.

A. Deadlock avoidance 

MESH KV splits traffic across two virtual networks: VN0 carries unicast PART reductions on dimension-ordered XY routing, while VN1 carries KV_FETCH and KV_DATA multicast on XYM-restricted turns [18]. No flit holds a VN0 buffer while waiting for VN1 resources or vice versa, so inter-network cycles are structurally eliminated; restricted-turn routing removes intra-network cycles within each VN. B. Energy efficiency 

At T =32K , M ESH KV reduces link energy by 48% relative to C ENT : T AKV shortens paths and M ARE eliminates duplicate KV copies, in line with the 58% traffic cut in Table IV. SRAM energy increases 7% because T AKV reads from distributed banks rather than one contiguous array. Net energy per token decreases by 17 % under the activity-based estimate in §VIII. Shared multicast further amortizes the link term at B> 1, where concurrent streams reuse the same KV_DATA tree. 

C. Comparison with prior FPGA decoders 

FPGA LLM accelerators DFX [20] and FlightLLM [21] optimize end-to-end execution and HBM/BRAM management; CXL-SpecKV [22] speculates KV on a disaggregated FPGA. MESH KV implements the on-chip communication substrate for distributed KV movement, not a full FFN-and-norm decoder. Different platforms, mappings, memory systems, and datapaths therefore make absolute latencies incomparable. We report hardware measurements against C ENT and S HARED on the same 8×8 configuration: 58% less interconnect traffic, 2.1×

KV bandwidth utilization over S HARED , and up to 1.9×

throughput at B=8 (Tables IV–V, Figure 5).

X. C ONCLUSION 

MESH KV recasts decode KV movement as packetized mesh flows via T AKV striping, M ARE multicast, and credit-aligned PAD . Hardware measurements on FPGA datapaths show up to 58% less interconnect traffic, 2.1× KV bandwidth utilization, and 1.9× multi-stream throughput relative to same-platform baselines. As context lengths grow, M ESH KV is a promising communication-aware KV fabric for long-context decode on tiled accelerators. REFERENCES [1] A. Vaswani, N. Shazeer, N. Parmar, J. Uszkoreit, L.
